# Recording test clips for quality checks

Last updated: 2026-11-11. For the speakers. Part of the audio-quality experiments
([handover.md](handover.md)).

## Why we need these clips

We need about 360 short voice notes (about 2 hours) from 10 speakers, recorded on purpose in good
and, mostly, poor conditions. Raters will score them for audio quality, so our automatic checks
learn what noise, distance, echo and silence sound like after WhatsApp has processed them. The clips
are not part of the main speech dataset; with your permission, they may be published as an open
Chichewa audio-quality dataset.

## Before you start

- **Who:** 10 people, 2 each whose first language is Chichewa, Tumbuka, Sena, Yao and one more
  language (to be confirmed). Each person uses their own phone. Mostly budget Android phones like
  contributors use (Tecno, Itel, Infinix), a couple of mid-range ones (e.g. Samsung) and at most 2
  iPhones; the two speakers of each language use different brands.
- **When:** one day, date to be confirmed. Recording takes about 12 minutes; allow about an hour in
  total.
- **Consent:** you sign a short form first, including whether your clips may be published in an
  open dataset.
- **Your number must be registered as a tester.** Send your WhatsApp number to Dunstan first, with
  your name, first language and phone (brand and model). Recordings from unregistered numbers are
  not kept.
- **Use the normal bot:** save +1 618-602-0282 and send "Hi". Record with the WhatsApp microphone
  button, as a contributor would. Don't forward or attach audio files.
- **Any task is fine** (sentences, pictures, questions or free speech): the condition matters, not
  the task.

## What to record

**Each person records 36 clips (about 12 minutes): 3 in each of the 11 categories below, plus 3
extra good ones.** Speak Chichewa. Keep speech clips 10–30 seconds and silence clips 10–15 seconds.

| Code | Category | How to record it | Clips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C1 | Good | Quiet room, phone about 20 cm from your mouth, normal voice | 6 |
| C2 | Too quiet | Normal distance, speaking very softly | 3 |
| C3 | Far away | Phone on a table 1–2 metres away, normal voice | 3 |
| C4 | Background voices | TV or radio talk, or people chatting nearby | 3 |
| C5 | Background music | Radio, music from a phone, church singing | 3 |
| C6 | Noisy surroundings | Generator, engine, traffic, dishes, animals | 3 |
| C7 | Wind | Speaking outside on a windy day | 3 |
| C8 | Echo | Empty room, bathroom or large hall | 3 |
| C9 | Too close or muffled | Shouting into the phone, or hand or cloth over the microphone | 3 |
| C10 | Silence, quiet room | Press record and say nothing | 3 |
| C11 | Silence with background noise | Say nothing while TV, traffic or people can be heard | 3 |
|  | **Total** |  | **36** |

Most clips are deliberately poor: contributors already give us plenty of good ones. The 6 good clips
let us compare each phone in good and poor conditions.

## How to record

1. Dunstan tells you your order: forwards (C1 to C11) or backwards (C11 to C1).
2. Send "Hi" and start the task the bot offers.
3. **Before each category, send its code as a text message** (C1, C2 … C11). This labels the clips
   that follow.
4. Record that category's clips (see the table). For C10 and C11, press record and say nothing.
5. If the bot asks for a retake or says it heard nothing, just record the next clip: every attempt
   is kept.
6. If the bot says you have reached your target before you finish, tell Dunstan; he can raise it.

Finish one category before starting the next, and always send its code first.
